# 9. Sustaining the Open-Source Guide

*How the guide stays alive after the project ends.*

## 9.1 What to Contribute

This guide is intended to be a living document. The field of digital inclusion for women entrepreneurs in sub-Saharan Africa is moving quickly. User behaviours are changing as smartphone penetration increases, the product landscape is evolving, and local language technology is improving. What holds true today about connectivity constraints may look different in a few years. The value of this guide over time depends on whether new practitioners engage with it, challenge it, and update it with their own evidence.

The most useful contributions are specific and evidence-based. If your project confirmed something in this guide, say so. If your findings contradict something, say that too, and explain the context: region, product type, and user demographics all matter.

**Priority contribution areas:**

- New case examples from similar projects in Ghana or elsewhere in the region.
- Updated data on connectivity, smartphone ownership, or digital literacy patterns.
- Additional screening templates, observation guides, or evaluation frameworks adapted for specific contexts.
- Lessons from product types or user groups not covered in this edition.
- Additional Phase 2 solutions and later Phase 2 cohorts not yet reflected in Section 4, Part B and Section 5.6.
- Translations of this guide or its appendices into local languages.
- Design patterns or component libraries tested with this user population.
- Validation evidence from AI-enabled solutions, particularly speech interfaces in Ghanaian languages, as Solutions S13, P8, P9, and P10 mature (see [Section 5.8](05-insights.md#58-ai-in-this-project-evidence-and-open-questions)).

## 9.2 Contribution Guidelines

Contributions to this guide should be practical and based on documented field activity, not general design theory; clearly referenced, stating what project, context, and evidence base the contribution draws on; aligned with user-centred design principles and gender-responsive practice; and submitted with transparency about limitations, presenting context-specific findings as such.

### Help improve OS Guide

Choose the route that best matches what you want to contribute. Each route is public and requires a free GitHub account.

1. **[Report a correction](https://github.com/joekay-kiipo/os-guide-site/issues/new?template=correction.yml).** Flag inaccurate or outdated information, unclear guidance, broken links, or editorial mistakes. Small, well-defined corrections are the easiest contributions to review.
2. **[Share field evidence or a case study](https://github.com/joekay-kiipo/os-guide-site/issues/new?template=field-evidence.yml).** Add findings that confirm, challenge, or extend the guide. The submission asks about context, participants, methods, findings, sources, and limitations so that future maintainers can assess it without extensive follow-up.
3. **Edit the guide directly.** Select **Edit this page** at the top of any guide page to propose a change to its Markdown source. GitHub will create a pull request; you cannot break the published guide by proposing an edit.
4. **[Volunteer to help maintain the guide](https://github.com/joekay-kiipo/os-guide-site/issues/new?template=maintainer.yml).** Practitioners and organizations can offer review, accessibility, research, translation, or technical stewardship.

Before opening an issue or pull request, search the [existing issues](https://github.com/joekay-kiipo/os-guide-site/issues) to see whether the same contribution is already being discussed.

!!! caution "Maintained on a best-effort basis"
    OS Guide is an open-source, community-maintained resource. Contributions are welcome, but response and review times depend on whether an active maintainer is available. Submission does not guarantee inclusion. If the project becomes inactive, anyone may continue the work through a fork or publish an adaptation under the licence in Section 9.5.

### Two conditions apply

Contributions are published under the same CC BY 4.0 licence as the rest of the guide, so please send only material you are free to share on those terms. Anything describing a participant, a business, or a solution provider must already be anonymised, or must come with that person's permission: the standard set out in Section 5.7 applies to contributions exactly as it applied to the original research.

### Contradictory findings are welcome

Findings that contradict this guide are among the most valuable contributions it can receive. If your evidence disagrees with something here, say so and explain the context: region, product type, and user demographics all matter.

## 9.3 Review and Governance Model

Ownership of this guide is intended to transfer from the GFA/GIZ Project team to a Ghana Hubs Network (GHN) open-source community as part of the project's sustainability plan. Until a standing maintainer group exists, the GitHub repository is the public source of truth for proposed changes, review discussion, accepted contributions, and project status.

Minor corrections may be merged by any active repository maintainer after a basic accuracy check. New research claims, statistics, case studies, or substantial changes should be supported by documented evidence and reviewed by someone with relevant subject knowledge before publication. Automated checks may verify links, formatting, and required submission fields, but they do not replace human judgement about evidence, safeguarding, or contextual accuracy.

Major updates should follow versioned releases; minor corrections and additions can be incorporated in interim updates. If no maintainer remains available, the repository should say so clearly and invite new maintainers. If long-term stewardship cannot be found, the repository may be archived as a read-only record while retaining the right for others to fork and adapt it under CC BY 4.0.

## 9.4 Knowledge-Sharing Platforms

This guide is intended to be distributed and built upon through a Git-based repository (GitHub or GitLab) for version-controlled collaborative editing; GHN community forums and practitioner networks focused on digital inclusion and gender-responsive design; workshops, learning sessions, and design clinics organized under GHN's knowledge-sharing agenda; and partner organization publications and distribution channels.

## 9.6 Sustaining This as a Revenue Stream for GHN Members

[Section 8](08-business-model.md) sets out how individual hubs can turn the capability this guide documents into a paid service. From a network-sustainability perspective, that is also what keeps this guide alive in practice: hubs that are actively running user testing engagements are the ones most likely to generate the new case studies, corrections, and Phase 2 content this guide will need (Section 9.1). Treat Section 8 and this section as two sides of the same goal: one hub's income, and the whole network's evidence base.

## 9.7 Applying the Guide Flexibly

No two projects are identical. The guidance here comes from a specific context: Ghana, 2023–2025, peri-urban and rural women micro-entrepreneurs. Before applying any recommendation elsewhere, ask what is the same and what is different. The principles tend to transfer; the specific implementation details often need adjustment.
